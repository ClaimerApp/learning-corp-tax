# Glossary

This glossary defines every key term used in the tutorial in plain English, ordered alphabetically. Use it two ways: read it through once after Week 1 to get the lay of the land, then dip back in whenever a bolded term appears in a weekly chapter. Where a figure or threshold matters, the current value as of 28 May 2026 is included. Confusable terms have a "Don't confuse with" note to keep them straight.

---

## A

### Accounting Period
The stretch of time your company's books cover. For a normal company this is twelve months long, ending on the **accounting reference date**. Acorn Studio Ltd's accounting period for Weeks 1 to 4 is 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026.

> **Don't confuse with:** the **CTAP** (Corporation Tax Accounting Period). They usually match, but in a first or shortened year they can differ.

### Accounting Reference Date (ARD)
The day your company's financial year ends. Set by Companies House when you incorporate (usually the last day of the month you incorporated in). Acorn Studio's ARD is 31 March. You can shorten or extend the ARD, with restrictions.

### Accruals
Costs your company has incurred in the period but hasn't yet received an invoice or paid for. You post them at year-end so the P&L shows the right cost for the period. Acorn Studio accrues its £900 accountancy fee at 31 March 2026 because the invoice arrives in May.

> **Don't confuse with:** **prepayments**, which are the opposite (you've paid for something that relates to the next period).

### Add-back
A cost that's in the P&L but isn't allowed for tax purposes, so you add it back to accounting profit when working out taxable profit. Depreciation and business entertaining are the classic examples.

### Aged Receivables / Aged Payables
Reports out of Xero or QuickBooks showing how old your unpaid customer invoices (receivables) and supplier bills (payables) are, usually split into buckets of 30, 60, 90, 90+ days. Useful for spotting bad debts and cashflow problems.

### Annual Investment Allowance (AIA)
A capital allowance that lets you deduct the full cost of most qualifying plant and machinery from your taxable profit in the year of purchase. Currently set at **£1,000,000 per year** and permanent. Acorn Studio uses AIA on its £1,900 MacBook and desk purchase.

> **Don't confuse with:** **full expensing**, which gives the same 100% deduction but only for companies and only on new and unused main-pool assets, with no annual cap. AIA covers both new and second-hand assets and is available to companies, partnerships and sole traders.

### Associated Companies
Companies under common control. Matters because the corporation tax thresholds (£50,000 and £250,000) are divided by the number of associated companies plus one. If Sarah Brown also owned a separate Ltd, both companies would share the thresholds.

---

## B

### Balance Sheet
A snapshot at one moment in time of what the company owns (assets), what it owes (liabilities), and the residual that belongs to the shareholder (equity). Always balances: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. In FRS 105 micro-entity accounts it's called the "statement of financial position".

### Bank Reconciliation
The process of matching every line on your bank statement to the matching line in your accounting software, so you can prove that the bank balance in your books is the same as the bank's. In Xero this happens through the bank feed and "Reconcile" screen. Sarah does this weekly.

---

## C

### Capital Allowances
The tax system's substitute for depreciation. You can't deduct depreciation when working out taxable profit, but you can deduct capital allowances on qualifying fixed asset purchases. The most common ones are **AIA**, **full expensing**, and **writing-down allowances (WDA)**.

### CATO (Company Accounts and Tax Online)
HMRC's free joint-filing service for tiny companies. It let you file a CT600 and basic accounts together with HMRC. **Closing on 31 March 2026.** From 1 April 2026 you must use commercial software for the CT600 and iXBRL accounts/computation.

### Chart of Accounts
The list of every account in your general ledger, organised into categories (income, cost of sales, expenses, assets, liabilities, equity). Xero, QuickBooks and FreeAgent come with a default chart that you can adjust.

### Common
Plain-English shorthand for "ordinary shares" the standard type of share most small UK Ltds issue. One common share, one vote, one share of any dividend.

### Companies House
The UK registrar of companies. Every Ltd files annual accounts and a confirmation statement with Companies House. Separate body from HMRC.

### Companies House WebFiling
The free online portal for filing accounts and confirmation statements with Companies House. Remains free at least until 1 April 2027. A planned software-only mandate has been delayed; the timing is under review as of January 2026.

### Confirmation Statement
A short annual filing that confirms Companies House's records about your company (directors, shareholders, registered office, PSC, SIC codes) are still correct. Filed within 14 days of the end of each 12-month "review period". Costs £34 online.

> **Don't confuse with:** statutory accounts. The confirmation statement is just a "yes, these details are still right" check, not a financial report.

### Corporation Tax
The tax a UK limited company pays on its taxable profits. Charged at the **small profits rate** of 19% up to £50,000 of taxable profit, the **main rate** of 25% above £250,000, with **marginal relief** between those thresholds.

### Corporation Tax Return (CT600)
The official return you file with HMRC showing your company's taxable profits and the corporation tax due. Due 12 months after the end of the accounting period. Acorn Studio's CT600 for the year ended 31 March 2026 is due by 31 March 2027.

### CTAP (Corporation Tax Accounting Period)
The period the CT600 covers. Normally identical to the accounting period in the company's books. Can split into two CTAPs if your accounting period is longer than 12 months.

---

## D

### Depreciation
The accounting way of spreading the cost of a fixed asset (like a laptop) over its useful life. It's a P&L cost. **It is not allowed for tax purposes**: capital allowances replace it.

> **Don't confuse with:** **capital allowances**. Depreciation lives in the accounts; capital allowances live in the tax computation.

### Director's Loan Account (DLA)
A ledger account that tracks money owed between the director and the company. If the director takes more out of the company than they're owed (in salary, dividends, or repayments of loans they made), the DLA is **overdrawn** and the company has lent the director money.

### Disallowable
A cost that sits in your P&L but isn't allowed as a deduction for corporation tax. Common examples: business entertainment, fines and penalties, most political donations, depreciation. You add these back when computing taxable profit.

### Dividend
A distribution of profit from a company to its shareholders. Must be paid out of distributable reserves (retained profits after tax). Sarah takes £25,000 in dividends from Acorn Studio in the year ended 31 March 2026.

> **Don't confuse with:** salary. Dividends are paid to shareholders out of taxed profit; salary is paid to employees as a deductible cost.

### Double Entry
The fundamental rule of bookkeeping: every transaction has at least two sides, a debit and an equal credit. Sarah pays £100 for software: software expense debit £100, bank credit £100.

---

## E

### ERIS (Enhanced R&D Intensive Support)
A more generous version of the R&D relief regime for loss-making SMEs whose R&D spend is at least 30% of total expenditure. Gives an 86% additional deduction and a 14.5% payable credit on surrendered losses. Acorn Studio does not qualify.

---

## F

### Filleted Accounts
The cut-down version of small or micro-entity accounts that you can choose to file with Companies House. The full version (with director's report and full P&L) is what shareholders and HMRC get; the filleted version that goes to Companies House is shorter and skips the P&L. Saves a small amount of privacy.

### FRS 102 Section 1A
The "small company regime" reporting standard for UK companies that exceed the micro-entity thresholds but are still small. From 6 April 2025, the small company thresholds are: turnover £15m, balance sheet £7.5m, employees 50 (meet any two of three).

### FRS 105
The micro-entity reporting standard. The simplest UK reporting regime. From 6 April 2025, thresholds are: turnover £1m, balance sheet £500k, employees 10 (meet any two of three). Acorn Studio Ltd uses FRS 105.

### Full Expensing
A 100% first-year allowance on new and unused main-pool plant and machinery. Permanent, companies only, no annual cap. Useful if you ever exceed the £1m AIA limit.

### FY2025
"Financial Year 2025" in tax-speak runs from **1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026.** UK corporation tax financial years are named after the calendar year they start in. FY2026 runs 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027.

> **Don't confuse with:** a company's own accounting period (sometimes called its financial year), which can end on any date.

---

## G

### General Ledger
The complete record of every accounting transaction the company has ever posted, organised by account. Everything in the P&L, balance sheet and trial balance ultimately comes from here.

### Government Gateway
The HMRC and gov.uk login system. You need a Government Gateway account (and the right HMRC services added to it) to file the CT600, the company VAT return, and to set up the company for PAYE.

---

## H

### HMRC
His Majesty's Revenue and Customs. The UK tax authority. Receives your CT600, your VAT return, and your PAYE submissions.

---

## I

### iXBRL
"Inline eXtensible Business Reporting Language." A format that's part-XML, part-HTML used to tag the numbers and narrative in your statutory accounts and tax computation so HMRC's computers can read them. Commercial filing software does this for you automatically.

---

## L

### Limited Company
A company whose shareholders' liability is limited to the amount they've paid (or agreed to pay) for their shares. Most UK Ltds are private companies limited by shares ("Ltd" rather than "PLC"). Separate legal person from its owner.

---

## M

### Main Rate
The headline corporation tax rate of **25%**, paid on taxable profits above **£250,000**.

### Making Tax Digital (MTD)
HMRC's programme to require digital record-keeping and quarterly submissions. **MTD for Corporation Tax has been confirmed as not going ahead** (HMRC Transformation Roadmap, July 2025). MTD for ITSA applies to sole traders and landlords from April 2026 but **does not apply to limited companies**. MTD for VAT does apply if you're VAT-registered.

### Marginal Relief
The mechanism that smooths the transition between the 19% small profits rate and the 25% main rate. Applies to taxable profits between £50,001 and £250,000. Uses the fraction 3/200. The effective rate on profit in that band is **26.5%**. HMRC publishes a calculator at https://www.tax.service.gov.uk/marginal-relief-calculator.

### Micro-entity
A company small enough to qualify for **FRS 105** reporting. From 6 April 2025: turnover £1m, balance sheet £500k, ten or fewer employees (meet any two of three). Acorn Studio comfortably qualifies.

---

## N

### NIC (National Insurance Contributions)
A separate UK payroll tax. Paid by employees ("primary"), employers ("secondary"), and the self-employed. Acorn Studio pays no employer NIC in Weeks 1 to 4 because Sarah's £12,570 salary is below the secondary threshold for 2025/26. Adds an employee in Week 5, which triggers employer NIC.

### Nominal Account
Old-school term for any single account in the general ledger, e.g. "Travel" or "Bank". Each nominal account has a number ("nominal code") in many software packages.

---

## P

### PAYE (Pay As You Earn)
The system for collecting income tax and NIC from employees' salaries. A Ltd that pays even a single director's salary must register for PAYE and file submissions to HMRC every time it runs payroll.

### Profit and Loss (P&L)
The financial statement showing income minus costs over the period, giving the profit (or loss) for the period. Sometimes called the "income statement". Acorn Studio's P&L for the year ended 31 March 2026 shows £74,800 of turnover and £47,365 of profit before tax.

### Prepayments
The opposite of accruals. You've paid for something in this period that relates to the next period. At year-end you move the future-period portion off the P&L and onto the balance sheet. Acorn Studio prepays £240 for the next year of an annual software sub.

---

## R

### R&D (Research and Development)
A specific tax regime giving extra relief for genuine scientific or technological advance. From accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, most companies use the **merged scheme** giving a 20% above-the-line credit (RDEC-style). Loss-making R&D-intensive SMEs may qualify for **ERIS** instead.

### RDEC (Research and Development Expenditure Credit)
The "above-the-line" R&D credit. From 1 April 2024 the rate is 20% under the merged scheme.

---

## S

### s.455
Section 455 of the Corporation Tax Act 2010. A penal tax of **33.75%** (rising to **35.75% from 6 April 2026**) on any overdrawn director's loan balance still outstanding 9 months and 1 day after the period end. Reclaimable when the loan is repaid. Sarah triggers an s.455 charge in Week 5.

### Small Profits Rate
The lower corporation tax rate of **19%**, charged on taxable profits up to **£50,000**.

### Sole Director
A director who is the only director of the company. Common in micro Ltds. Sarah is the sole director (and the sole shareholder) of Acorn Studio Ltd.

### Statutory Accounts
The formal year-end accounts a company must produce by law (under the Companies Act 2006) and file with Companies House and submit alongside its CT600. For Acorn Studio, the FRS 105 micro-entity format applies.

---

## T

### Trial Balance (TB)
A list of every account in the general ledger and its balance at a point in time, with debits and credits in two columns. If the books are right, the two columns are equal. The trial balance is the bridge from your bookkeeping to your statutory accounts and tax computation.

---

## U

### UTR (Unique Taxpayer Reference)
A 10-digit number HMRC issues to your company. You need it to file the CT600 and to pay corporation tax. Companies and individuals each have their own separate UTRs.

> **Don't confuse with:** the **Companies House number** (8 digits, issued at incorporation), or your **VAT number** (9 digits, issued only if VAT-registered).

---

## V

### VAT (Value Added Tax)
A consumption tax charged on most goods and services. Standard rate 20%. You must register if your taxable turnover crosses **£90,000** in any rolling 12-month period (threshold from 1 April 2024). Acorn Studio registers part-way through Week 5.

---

## W

### WDA (Writing-Down Allowance)
The annual capital allowance on the unused portion of your fixed asset pool. **Main pool: 18% reducing balance.** **Special rate pool: 6% reducing balance.** Cars use WDA based on CO2 emissions; zero-emission cars get a 100% first-year allowance.
